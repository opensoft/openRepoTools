# `/lane-swap` — the alias of `/handoff`

**Invoke the `handoff` skill now and follow every step of it, in order, to the end.**

That is the whole of this file. Lane-collision-protocol **Amendment 17(a)**, in force 2026-09-14T09:45:33Z:
*"The act Amendment 8(a) calls the swap is the HANDOFF: `lane-handoff` on `PATH` … from a shell, `/handoff`
from inside a session; `/lane-swap` and `/swap` remain as aliases of `/handoff` so that nothing written
about them stops working."*

The steps are **not restated here**, and that is the amendment's own reasoning rather than a preference: two
copies of one procedure that must stay byte-equal is the alternative clause (g) rejects by name, and it is
why `commands/swap.md` has been a pointer since A11 Addendum 4 ruling 9. One skill is the source of the act;
`/handoff`, `/swap`, `/lane-swap` and `lane-handoff` are four doors to it, and they write the same record.

**What differs between a swap and a handoff is only WHY**, and the record's free text has always carried
that — `clear`, `reset`, `switch`, `handoff to <who>`, `requested by <uuid>@<host>/<container>`. Nothing else
changes with the why. If what you meant was a context clear, the word is `/ctx` (`/handoff --restart`),
which is the same act with the restart attached.
